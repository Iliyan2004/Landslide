Infinite-slope stability with rain filling the soil, run as a 40-member ensemble (cohesion and depth ±25 %, friction ±3°). Only slopes of 10–50° are treated as soil-covered, and snow, ice and open water are excluded using ESA WorldCover. Steeper cells are rock and are excluded, as are cells already unstable before rain. With the root-cohesion button on, WorldCover adds assumed root cohesion (trees 5, shrub 3, grass 2, crop 1 kPa). Unit weight 18 kN/m³, porosity 0.4 and half the rain infiltrating are assumed. Cells are 160 m, so the map shows where the scenario fails, not individual landslides. WorldPop is smoothed over 100 m, so it can put people on steep cells that hold no buildings. With an earthquake selected, a pseudostatic horizontal force of 0.5 × the ShakeMap PGA (assumed) acts on every cell on top of the rain. The PGA is multiplied by an assumed amplification: crest cells (more than 40 m above their 800 m neighbourhood) take the crest factor and unconsolidated sediment takes the soft-ground factor. Building codes use factors of the order 1.2 to 1.4 for ridges (Eurocode 8, Part 5), but these are placeholders until site data exist. ShakeMap values are for average ground and do not include either effect.

Ground roughness scatters each bounce sideways, and block shape raises rolling resistance and lowers bounce (angular and platy blocks stop sooner); the shape multipliers are assumed. If the deposit blocks a river (HydroRIVERS reaches), the panel reports the dam height, the lake behind it, a stability index and a peak breach outflow, and the Breach flood button routes that outflow down the valley inside the window. The Uncertainty button repeats the replay 12 times with friction, soil depth, bounce and roughness varied, and shows how often each cell is reached plus ranges for run-out and deposit. The site replay runs a depth-averaged debris flow and bouncing rigid blocks on your 53 m DEM, draped on the map's 3D terrain. It stops at the time limit or when everything has settled, then shows the deposit, scar and rock rest positions. Both models are illustrative and not calibrated against any event.

Historical landslides: pick one to replay

Each replay rebuilds one reported landslide on your terrain: where it started, how it moved, its speed, and where the debris stopped. Only the date, type, trigger, size class and an approximate position are reported (NASA COOLR). The release zone, volume and friction are assumptions, so a replay is a reconstruction and not a record. Positions are often rounded to a village or a few kilometres, so "reached the reported place" is a weak test.
